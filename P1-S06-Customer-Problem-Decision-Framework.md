# P1-S06: Customer problem decision framework

**Status:** Decision criteria prepared; the decision itself is pending customer interviews.
**Purpose:** Decide whether to proceed with the Application Review Kit, revise the audience/problem, or test a different toolkit idea based on Phase 1 evidence rather than assumptions.

## Evidence required before deciding

- Complete at least five qualified interviews with people who personally participated in a recent application-security review; extend toward eight if evidence is mixed or key perspectives are missing.
- Summarize recent behavior, actual workflow, frequency, impact, current alternatives, workarounds, objections, and what already works.
- Count independent participants per theme; do not count repeated mentions by one person as additional evidence.
- Separate unprompted experience from responses after a probe or concept description.
- Include counterexamples and people for whom the hypothesized problem is not important.
- Use anonymous participant IDs and remove identifying or confidential details.

## Decision paths

### Proceed with the Application Review Kit hypothesis

Proceed to Phase 2 refinement and prototype planning only if:

- At least three independent participants in the discovery sample describe substantially the same meaningful problem without being led.
- Their concrete examples show recurring frequency, material effort/coordination, or a meaningful consequence—not only general interest in security.
- Existing tools and processes leave a specific gap the proposed workflow-support toolkit could address.
- The selected AppSec-reviewer-at-a-small-team audience remains coherent enough to describe.
- No unresolved objection or safety concern makes the proposed scope unsuitable.

This is a practical discovery gate for a small qualitative sample, not statistical proof of market demand. Continue to validate during prototype testing.

### Revise the audience or problem

Revise rather than proceed unchanged if interviewees share a need but:

- The person who experiences it is a different role, seniority, or team type.
- The strongest difficulty is a different part of the review workflow than the current draft assumes.
- Existing alternatives are useful but fail in a specific, narrower situation.
- The candidate scope includes resources users do not need or omits a repeated essential handoff.

Write the revised hypothesis and identify what evidence would distinguish it before expanding the toolkit.

### Test another toolkit idea or stop this direction

Test another idea (or pause) if:

- Fewer than three participants independently describe a meaningful common problem after five qualified interviews and the added interviews do not resolve the pattern.
- Review work is infrequent or low-impact for the intended audience.
- Existing internal processes, tools, consultants, or templates already solve the problem adequately.
- The proposed templates do not fit how people actually work or introduce unacceptable privacy, security, licensing, or compatibility concerns.

Do not use positive responses to a concept pitch or hypothetical willingness to buy as a substitute for evidence of current behavior and unmet need.

## Decision record template

Complete after the interview synthesis:

- **Decision:** Proceed / Revise audience or problem / Test another toolkit / Pause
- **Interview sample:** Number qualified and completed; role and team-size range (no identifying details)
- **Most repeated unprompted need:** Theme, distinct participant IDs, and concrete examples
- **Frequency and impact:** What participants said about recurrence, effort, coordination, or consequence
- **Current alternatives:** What people use and where it succeeds or falls short
- **Counterevidence and objections:** Who does not have the problem, what works well, and reasons to reject the kit
- **Scope implications:** Candidate contents to keep, remove, combine, or explore
- **Confidence and unknowns:** What the sample cannot establish
- **Next test:** Smallest evidence-gathering action and the result that would change the decision
