# CAB Synergy Project Plan

## Project outcome

Validate and launch one useful CAB Synergy toolkit before investing in the full catalog. The website remains a concept storefront until the product, pricing, fulfillment, and customer policies are confirmed.

## Recommended pilot

Use the **Application Review Kit** as the first candidate because its audience, task, and proposed contents are already described on the site. Treat this as a starting hypothesis, not a final commitment. Confirm it with target users before building the complete product.

## Phase 1: Choose and validate the customer problem

**Goal:** Confirm who the first toolkit is for and that the problem is important enough to solve.

**Current checkpoint:** The Application Review Kit audience, workflow, interview materials, and proceed/revise/retarget criteria are working hypotheses. No customer interviews have been completed; do not record a customer-problem decision or treat Phase 1 as validated.

Steps:
1. List the likely customer roles for the Application Review Kit, such as application security engineers and software teams.
2. Choose one primary audience and describe its review workflow, current tools, and pain points.
3. Speak with 5–8 people who match that audience. Ask about their current process, where work slows down, what they already use, and what would make a toolkit useful.
4. Avoid pitching the proposed product during the first part of each conversation; learn how customers work today.
5. Summarize repeated needs, objections, existing alternatives, and language customers use.
6. Decide whether to proceed, revise the audience/problem, or test another toolkit idea.

**Deliverable:** A one-page customer/problem brief.

**Exit check:** Proceed only when multiple target users independently describe the problem and the proposed toolkit addresses a clear gap.

## Phase 2: Define the minimum useful toolkit

**Goal:** Specify a small, coherent first version that solves one customer task.

**Current checkpoint:** A provisional product brief may be drafted in parallel with Phase 1 to make discovery more concrete. Treat its promise, contents, workflow, audience details, and safeguards as hypotheses until target users review them. Keep customer-dependent choices such as final formats open, and do not treat the draft as approval to build or sell.

Steps:
1. Write a one-sentence product promise focused on a practical task, not a guaranteed security outcome.
2. Define the intended user, prerequisites, and situations where the toolkit is not suitable.
3. Select only the worksheets, checklists, and templates necessary to complete the task.
4. Describe the expected workflow: what the user provides, what they do with each resource, and what they finish with.
5. Choose the file formats based on how customers already work.
6. Identify any security, privacy, licensing, accessibility, or compatibility considerations.

**Deliverable:** A product brief, table of contents, and acceptance checklist.

**Exit check:** A target user can understand what the toolkit does, who it is for, and what it does not promise.

## Phase 3: Build and test a prototype

**Goal:** Check whether the resources are clear and useful before polishing or expanding them.

Steps:
1. Create a rough but complete prototype of the minimum useful toolkit.
2. Use fictional or sanitized examples; do not include customer confidential information.
3. Ask 3–5 target users to complete a realistic task with the prototype.
4. Observe where they hesitate, misunderstand instructions, or need information that is missing.
5. Ask what they would change, what they would not use, and what they expected to be included.
6. Revise the toolkit and repeat the test for any major workflow changes.
7. Check that examples are clearly labeled and do not imply guaranteed security, compliance, or business results.

**Deliverable:** A tested toolkit prototype and a prioritized feedback log.

**Exit check:** Test users can complete the intended task without step-by-step help, and important gaps have been resolved or documented.

## Phase 4: Confirm the offer and price

**Goal:** Turn the tested prototype into an honest, supportable offer.

Steps:
1. Finalize the deliverables, formats, version, setup requirements, and compatibility notes.
2. Estimate creation, maintenance, support, and delivery costs.
3. Ask target users about the value and price expectations; compare these with costs and alternatives.
4. Set an initial price only after confirming the product and delivery method.
5. Define what customers receive, how updates work, what support is included, and any usage limits.
6. Decide whether bundles make sense after the first individual toolkit is validated.

**Deliverable:** A confirmed product specification, pricing rationale, and fulfillment checklist.

**Exit check:** CAB Synergy can reliably deliver exactly what the offer describes and support the stated terms.

## Phase 5: Prepare business and customer operations

**Goal:** Make it possible to collect interest or fulfill purchases responsibly.

Steps:
1. Choose an email/signup service before collecting customer addresses.
2. Explain what information is collected, why it is collected, how it will be used, and how a person can unsubscribe or request removal.
3. Select a payment and delivery method before enabling checkout.
4. Prepare accurate contact, privacy, licensing, refund, support, and delivery information for the business model.
5. Confirm relevant tax, consumer-protection, and other legal obligations with qualified professionals where needed.
6. Create a process for customer questions, delivery issues, corrections, and product updates.

**Deliverable:** A documented customer operations checklist and selected service providers.

**Exit check:** Signup and checkout remain disabled until their data handling, policies, and fulfillment workflows are ready.

## Phase 6: Update and test the website

**Goal:** Make the site accurately represent the confirmed product and make the next action clear.

Steps:
1. Replace concept descriptions with the validated audience, task, contents, and limitations.
2. Keep proposed information visibly labeled until it is confirmed; remove placeholder prices when they are no longer appropriate.
3. Make product previews clearly distinguish illustrative examples from actual downloadable deliverables.
4. Keep toolkit search, category filters, and comparison instructions understandable and test them with users.
5. Add a signup link only after the signup service and privacy explanation are ready.
6. Add checkout only after payment, delivery, support, and customer policies are ready.
7. Test keyboard access, screen-reader labels, color contrast, mobile layouts, page speed, links, and error states.
8. Keep search indexing disabled while the site is a concept; review metadata and enable indexing when the public offer is ready.

**Deliverable:** A tested, accurate product page and launch-ready site.

**Exit check:** A first-time visitor can explain who the toolkit is for, what it includes, its availability, and what action they can take.

## Phase 7: Launch and learn

**Goal:** Release the first confirmed toolkit, observe real customer use, and improve it.

Steps:
1. Run a final purchase-to-delivery test before announcing availability.
2. Confirm product files, pricing, terms, support contact, and confirmation messages match the site.
3. Announce availability to the validated audience using appropriate channels.
4. Track questions, drop-off points, delivery problems, refunds, and customer feedback.
5. Review feedback on a regular schedule and prioritize changes by customer impact.
6. Update version notes and notify customers when material changes are made.
7. Decide whether to improve the first toolkit, build the next individual toolkit, or create a bundle.

**Deliverable:** A launched product with a feedback and maintenance process.

**Exit check:** Fulfillment is reliable and customer feedback supports the next investment.

## Suggested first milestone

Complete **Phase 1** before building all eight proposed products, creating checkout, or publishing final prices. Keep the current website clearly marked as a concept until the launch-readiness checks in Phases 4–6 are complete.

Progress percentages alone do not pass a phase gate. When a task or exit criterion is finished, share its task ID and supporting deliverable/evidence so the same workbook and its dependent views can be updated.
