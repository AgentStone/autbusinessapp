# P1-S05: Interview synthesis framework

**Purpose:** Summarize repeated customer needs, objections, existing alternatives, and participants' language after discovery interviews.

**Current state:** Framework prepared; no interviews completed and no findings established. The candidate topics below are prompts drawn from the unvalidated P1-S02 hypotheses. They are not evidence or conclusions.

## Evidence rules

1. Synthesize only interviews with people who personally participated in a recent application-security review and fit the audience being investigated.
2. Use anonymous participant IDs (`P1`–`P8`). Count each participant at most once per theme, even if they mention it several times.
3. Separate a participant's direct description or quote from the researcher's interpretation and product implication.
4. Mark whether a theme came up unprompted or only after a question/probe. Do not treat agreement after prompting as an independently discovered need.
5. Include contradictory evidence, useful existing processes, and participants who do not experience the hypothesized problem.
6. Do not infer demand from politeness, enthusiasm for the concept, or a hypothetical willingness to buy. Give more weight to recent behavior, repeated frequency, impact, workarounds, and current spending/effort.
7. Remove names, employers, customer details, vulnerabilities, and other confidential or identifying information from the synthesis.

## Candidate topics to test (not findings)

Use these only as coding prompts after collecting participant accounts. Add new themes if interviews reveal something unexpected.

- **Review preparation and context:** How the reviewer establishes scope, ownership, data flows, and necessary evidence.
- **Review consistency and coverage:** Whether the work depends on an individual's memory or existing repeatable guidance.
- **Evidence and decision traceability:** How the team records evidence, rationale, and review decisions.
- **Severity and recommendations:** How impact, priority, and practical next steps are agreed.
- **Remediation ownership and follow-up:** How findings become owned work and how fixes are verified.
- **Existing process sufficiency:** Internal standards, people, tools, or templates that already work well or make a new toolkit unnecessary.
- **Audience fit and review cadence:** Who performs reviews, what triggers them, and how often.
- **Unexpected themes:** Any repeated need, objection, workaround, or context not represented above.

## Per-theme synthesis

For every theme with evidence, capture:

- **Theme and concise need:** State what people need to accomplish, not a proposed feature.
- **Participant IDs and count:** List distinct interview IDs; never count repeated mentions by one participant twice.
- **Unprompted count:** Number of those participants who raised the theme before a leading probe or concept description.
- **Evidence:** Summarize concrete recent examples and note whether they describe behavior, opinion, or a hypothetical.
- **Participant language:** One short, anonymized verbatim phrase where permitted. Do not polish it into a marketing claim.
- **Frequency and impact:** How often it occurs and what time, coordination, risk, or other consequence the participant described.
- **Current alternative/workaround:** What they already do, including tools, internal process, or a deliberate choice to do nothing.
- **Counterevidence/objections:** Participants for whom this is not a problem, what already works, and reasons a new toolkit would not fit.
- **Researcher interpretation and implication:** Keep separate from the evidence and mark as a hypothesis.
- **Confidence and next question:** Note sample limitations and what to ask or test next.

## Decision checkpoint

The interview target is 5–8 qualified participants. After five, review whether at least three independent participants have described the same meaningful problem without being led, whether it has material frequency or impact, and whether existing alternatives leave a clear gap. This is a discovery gate, not statistical proof. If evidence is mixed, continue toward eight or narrow/revise the audience instead of presenting a weak pattern as validated.

Do not mark P1-S05 complete until interviews exist and this synthesis contains evidence, counts, alternatives, language, and counterevidence. No customer interview results have been supplied as of October 2, 2026.
